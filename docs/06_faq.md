# Frequently asked questions

## What does a SMART entry represent?

One entry represents one metabolite-change observation. A single publication can contribute multiple entries.

## Does a metabolite appearing in more tissues mean that it is more important?

Not necessarily. Cross-source recurrence can be useful evidence, but it is also affected by study number, analytical method, sample availability, and reporting practices.

## Does absence from a tissue/source mean that the metabolite is absent there?

No. It means that the corresponding stress-associated observation was not identified in the literature curated in the current SMART release.

## Can I directly compare metabolite counts between tissues?

Use caution. Raw counts are influenced by literature coverage and analytical-platform heterogeneity and should not be treated as quantitative measurements of tissue responsiveness.

## Why are metabolite synonyms provided?

Different studies may report the same metabolite using abbreviations, trivial names, spelling variants, acid/base forms, or different naming conventions. The synonym table makes the harmonization process inspectable.

## Why do some metabolites lack HMDB, KEGG, or PubChem identifiers?

Some entries are ratios, composite measures, unresolved features, or names for which an unambiguous mapping was not available.

## What is the difference between SMART and HMDB/KEGG/PubChem?

SMART is a stress-centered literature evidence resource. HMDB, KEGG, and PubChem provide broader chemical, biochemical, pathway, or compound information and are used as complementary annotation resources.

## How should I propose a correction?

Open a GitHub issue using the data-correction template and provide the affected metabolite/study, the current value, the proposed correction, and supporting evidence.
